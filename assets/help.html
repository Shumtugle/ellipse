Help

How Ellipse works, part by part. Almost everything is reached in two ways: by holding a thing on the home screen, or from the settings, which open from the home screen's own menu.

THE HOME SCREEN

The screens stand side by side; swipe across to turn them. One of them is the home screen, ringed among the points at the foot; the Home button brings it back. Hold an empty place for the home screen's menu: add a screen, a widget, a shortcut, a folder, make this screen the home one, or open the settings.

With endless scrolling on, the last screen is followed by the first, as if the screens were a ring.

ICONS, FOLDERS AND WIDGETS

Holding a thing opens its menu: an app's own shortcuts first, then what the home screen can do to it — change its icon, put it in a category, uninstall it, resize it, remove it. Keep the finger moving instead, and the thing is carried: set it down anywhere free, on another screen, in the dock, or on a folder to put it in.

Folders are made from the home screen's menu, or by setting an app down on a folder. A large folder shows its apps as a small page of its own; resize it from its menu.

Widgets come from the widget shelf. The phone asks once whether Ellipse may make widgets; allow it always, and widgets are placed without asking again. A widget is resized from its menu, and may wear a frame of the icons' materials.

Half steps let a thing stand half a place along or down. Overlap lets things lie over one another; the last set down is on top, and a thing's menu can bring it to the front or send it behind.

THE DOCK

A bar of four at the foot of every screen, and the way into every app. Carry an app onto it to put it there, or off it to take it away. It can be hidden in the Desktop settings.

ALL APPS

Every app, in lines going down or in pages going across. The bar at the foot searches as you write; its menu sorts the list — from A to Z, the newest first, or the most lately updated. Hold an app to carry it onto a screen.

Categories put tabs across the list: none; kinds you name and fill yourself, from an app's menu; or the kinds the apps say they are. An app you move yourself stays where you put it. The list's background may be the theme's own or any colour, bright or dim, solid or clear.

Hidden apps are left out of the list and its search; they are chosen in the settings.

WHAT IS FRESH

The Back gesture on the home screen lowers a panel with three tabs: the apps opened most from here, those opened last, and those installed or updated lately — each with a word saying which. Push it back up to close it.

THE CLOCK

The home screen's own clock is a thing of the grid: hold it to resize it or move it. It has four faces — the first, in the colours of the theme; the plate; the glass card; and the rings — each with its own settings for colours and the size of its windows, and a way back to the face as it was drawn. A tap on the clock's parts opens the alarm, the calendar, the weather or the phone's charge.

The rings are glass circles: a big one with the hour, and up to seven small ones, each holding what you choose — the time, the weather, the calendar, the charge, how warm it feels, the humidity, the wind, the daylight, the rain, the next alarm. A ring for the headphones joins them while they are connected. From the clock's menu, Arrange rings lets the fingers move them and pinch them to size.

THE WEATHER

A tap on the weather opens its page: the sky now and in the hours to come, the days ahead, the wind, the air, pollen, the sun, and the chance of an aurora. The place is chosen in the settings, or is where the phone roughly is.

THE LOOK

Theme: dark, light, or as the phone is set. Colour and text: the accent, the ground, how solid the cards are. Icons: the outline every icon is cut to, from circles to the paper tile, the chrome plate and the sticker; the rim, glass and gloss of the materials; the size of icons and their names; packs of icons, whose own pictures, or whose ground, mask and gloss, the icons take. Typeface: the letters of every name and every word.

Presets dress the home screen at a touch. Material 3 puts it back as it first came: icons in the phone's own shape and colours, the first clock, no rims or frames. Wood, Steel, Meno, Rings, Paper, Walnut and Gold each dress it whole: the icons' outline and rim, the clock and its case, the widgets' frames, the accent. Your own can be kept under a name. A preset never touches what you set out yourself — the grids, the apps and their places, the names under the icons, the theme — and the look worn before can be put back.

THE GRID

The Desktop settings choose the columns and rows, the shape of a place — the screen's share, square, wide or tall — the height of the rows, and how the grid meets the screen's edges: within its margins, widgets past them, or no margins at all. The phone's status bar and navigation bar may be hidden over the home screen; a swipe from the edge shows them for a moment.

GESTURES

A swipe up or down, two fingers swept up or down, a pinch, a double tap on the empty home screen, the Back gesture and the Home button can each be given anything: nothing, all apps, the search for apps, the notifications, the quick settings, what is fresh, the home screen, locking the phone, these settings, or any app you choose.

The phone is locked in one of two ways. Through an accessibility service it locks at once; Ellipse reads nothing on the screen, but some apps, a bank's for one, refuse to open while such a service from outside the phone's store is on. As a keeper of the phone, allowed to lock it and nothing more, those apps do not mind — only the first unlock after may ask for the code rather than a finger.

BACKUPS

Back up into a file keeps every screen, the dock, the folders, the clock, every setting and every kept look — with a language brought from a file and the pictures icons were given — in one file, saved wherever you choose. Send a copy hands the same file to whatever the phone sends with — a cloud, a mail, a chat. Restore from a file brings it back; what was there is first set aside, so the restore can be undone. Each time a new version starts, the set-out the old one left is kept too, the last five of them.

From another home screen: the backups of some other home screens can be brought in — their screens, apps at their places, folders, shortcuts and links to pages or contacts, widgets where the phone allows, and much of how they looked. Apps that are not on this phone wait grey in their places under their names: a touch finds them in the store, and once installed they stand there themselves. The dress of this home screen is let go of first, so the look brought in is not laid over an old one. Widgets the phone does not let Ellipse make at once are named, to be added again.

Widgets belong to the phone. A copy remembers whose each widget is; when it is restored on a phone that no longer holds them — after Ellipse is installed anew, or on another phone — they are made again in their places, if Ellipse is allowed to make widgets always. Those the phone asks about first are named, to be added again from the shelf.

LANGUAGES

English, Finnish and Russian come with Ellipse. Any other language can be made: save the English template, put every phrase after = into the language, by hand or with an assistant, and bring the file in. A phrase a module lacks is said in English.

WHEN SOMETHING IS WRONG

Last error keeps where and why the home screen last stopped; copy it and send it as words. Restart launcher closes the home screen and opens it again. Reset launcher forgets everything set by hand — make a backup first. If a version breaks something, the copy it kept of the version before is in Backup and restore.
